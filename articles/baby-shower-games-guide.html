Printable Baby Shower Games: How to Pick Winners (and Skip the Groans)

Hosting help from Pitter & Pat

Every host has the same worry: will the games be fun, or will guests smile politely while checking the time? The good news is that baby shower games follow a simple pattern — the ones that work are easy to start, quick to play, and let guests be a little bit clever or a little bit sentimental. Here's how to choose well.

How many games do you actually need?

For a typical two-to-three-hour shower, plan on 4–6 active games, plus 2–3 "set it out and let it run" activities like a diaper raffle or a prediction card guests fill in as they arrive. That's it. A bundle with 30+ games isn't about playing all of them — it's about having the right mix for your crowd, with spares when one game runs short or the energy dips.

The games guests genuinely love

  • Baby bingo — played during gift opening, so nobody is just watching. Always a hit.
  • The Price Is Right (baby edition) — guessing the cost of diapers and wipes gets surprisingly competitive.
  • Name That Baby Animal — quick, funny, and everyone can play at once from their seat.
  • Prediction cards — guests guess baby's birthday, weight, and name favorites; the parents keep them forever.
  • Wishes for baby — the sweet one. Two minutes of quiet writing that mom-to-be will reread for years.
  • Diaper raffle — not exactly a game, but the highest-participation activity at any shower. Guests bring a pack of diapers for a raffle ticket; the nursery starts stocked.

Ones to think twice about

Anything that requires guests to get messy, eat mystery baby food, or be put on the spot in front of people they've just met. If a game only works when everyone is a very good sport, it's the wrong game for a mixed crowd of coworkers, cousins, and grandma. Seat-friendly, pen-and-paper games scale to any group — and they're exactly what printables do best.

A running order that works

  • As guests arrive: prediction cards and the diaper raffle — no announcement needed.
  • After everyone settles: one fast group game (baby animal names or a word scramble) to warm up the room.
  • Mid-party: your feature game — The Price Is Right or a trivia round — while energy is highest.
  • During gifts: baby bingo, so opening presents has a game woven through it.
  • To close: wishes for baby — a sweet, calm note to end on.

Printing tips from hosts who've done it

Print one game card per guest, plus five spares — someone always loses theirs to a coffee ring. Cardstock feels special but regular paper is fine. Print a copy of the answer keys for yourself only, and bring more pens than you think you need. Pens walk away; it's a law of showers.
