Printable Bridal Shower Games: Crowd-Pleasers for Every Kind of Guest

Hosting help from Pitter & Pat

A bridal shower guest list is the hardest crowd in party hosting: the bride's college friends, her coworkers, her future mother-in-law, and a great-aunt who has strong opinions about all of it. The games that work for that mix are the ones everyone can play from their seat, that make the bride the star without embarrassing her, and that get the whole room laughing at the same answers. Here's how to pick them.

How many games do you actually need?

For a typical two-to-three-hour shower, plan on 4–6 active games, plus a couple of "set it out and let it run" activities — date night idea cards and an advice & wishes station guests can visit whenever there's a lull. A bundle with 35+ games isn't about playing all of them. It's about having the right game for every moment, with spares when one runs short.

The games guests genuinely love

  • He Said She Said — guests guess who said each quote, the bride or the groom. The wrong answers are the funniest part, and it works for guests who barely know the couple.
  • Would She Rather? — quick either/or questions about the bride's tastes. Fast, easy, and it starts conversations between guests who just met.
  • Who Knows the Bride Best? — the classic quiz. Expect friendly outrage when the college roommate loses to the bride's mom.
  • Bridal bingo — played during gift opening, so nobody is just watching the bride open a third serving platter.
  • Famous Love Quotes — match the quote to the movie or song. Romantic, low-key, and perfect while people are eating.
  • The Shoe Game — the one live game worth the chairs-in-a-circle effort: bride and groom back to back, swapping shoes to answer "who's more likely to…" questions. Save it for the finale — it's the moment everyone films.

Ones to think twice about

Anything that needs a pile of props, a lot of running around, or guests performing in front of people they've just met. And read the room on raunchy: a game that's hilarious with the bride's friends can land very differently with her grandmother at the same table. Seat-friendly, pen-and-paper games scale to any crowd — and they're exactly what printables do best.

A running order that works

  • As guests arrive: date night idea cards and the advice & wishes station — no announcement needed.
  • After everyone settles: a fast warm-up — a bridal word scramble or Would She Rather — to get the room talking.
  • Mid-party: your feature games — He Said She Said and Who Knows the Bride Best — while energy is highest.
  • During gifts: bridal bingo, so opening presents has a game woven through it.
  • To close: the Shoe Game — one live, laugh-out-loud finale with the couple center stage.

Printing tips from hosts who've done it

Print one game card per guest, plus five spares. Our bridal pages are designed on US Letter, and you can also print two pages per sheet and trim for classic 5×7 game cards. Print the signs — welcome, mimosa bar, cards & gifts — on cardstock so they stand up nicely. Keep the answer keys for yourself only, and bring more pens than you think you need. Pens walk away; it's a law of showers.
